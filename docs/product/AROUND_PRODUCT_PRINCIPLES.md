# AROUND Product Principles

## 1. Golf is where the journey starts

AROUND is not trying to build the biggest golf database.

The product should answer:

> Where is golf worth travelling for — and what makes the trip worth staying for?

Golf starts the journey. The surrounding place completes it.

## 2. Curated, not complete

A smaller set of strong recommendations is preferable to a long list of generic results.

Every place should ideally answer:
- Why this place?
- Why now / in this trip?
- What role does it play?
- How should it be planned?

## 3. PLAY / STAY / EAT / DO is the planning grammar

These four roles should remain understandable across:
- cards
- search
- destination pages
- trip planning
- recommendations

The role model is both editorial and functional.

## 4. AROUND should know how a place behaves

A Place is more useful when AROUND understands planning semantics.

Examples:
- a tee time behaves like a fixed point
- a restaurant reservation behaves like a fixed point
- an alpine area behaves flexibly
- a long hike occupies most of a day
- a hotel covers nights rather than hours

This is the beginning of planning intelligence.

## 5. Search is not the planner

Search / Discover is where a user explores.

The Planner is where a user commits choices to time.

Keep those mental models distinct.

Contextual Quick Add may bridge them without turning the planner into a search page.

## 6. The planner must remain editorially calm

Even with complex logic, the planner should not resemble enterprise scheduling software.

Preferred:
- strong typography
- clear time hierarchy
- concise controls
- contextual warnings
- progressive disclosure

Avoid:
- excessive status badges
- dense tables
- permanent filters everywhere
- red error-heavy UI

## 7. Warnings are guidance, not punishment

Examples:
- time conflicts
- uncovered nights
- incomplete planning

AROUND should say what is missing and provide an action.

It should not make users feel they have made an error simply because a trip is unfinished.

## 8. A trip can remain unfinished

A trip is allowed to be:
- exploratory
- partially planned
- missing a stay
- waiting for a reservation

AROUND should distinguish:
- unresolved
- intentionally not needed
- later decision
- complete

## 9. Mobile is a primary planning surface

The mobile bottom navigation and planner are first-class product surfaces.

Any planner change must be checked on:
- desktop wide
- intermediate desktop / tablet
- mobile portrait

## 10. Content pages should be visual

PLAY, STAY, EAT and DO pages should support multiple strong images.

The product should not force rich places into a single-image card/page model.

Recommended editorial media model:
- hero image
- gallery images
- card image or crop strategy
- captions / credits / alt text

## 11. Editorial identity matters

AROUND should not look like Booking.com, Google Maps or a generic itinerary app.

The UI can borrow useful interaction patterns, but the visual and editorial experience should remain recognizably AROUND.

## 12. Quality over feature count

After major workflow additions, prefer a dedicated polish / QA pass before starting another large feature.
